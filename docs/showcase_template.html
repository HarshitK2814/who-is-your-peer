Who Is Your Peer?

Vanguard's quantitative equity team told the SEC that its five-theme stock model ranks companies against their industry peers, and that "sector bets do not add value over the long term." I rebuilt the model from public filings, split its signal into a within-industry part and an industry-bet part, and tested the claim with the hypotheses frozen before the test period was run.

The claim survives, narrowly.Industry bets earned a test-period Sharpe of 0.68, but p = 0.085 after multiple-testing correction. The pre-registered verdict is "not contradicted".
A third of it was hidden beta.The "beta-neutral" industry book had a realised market beta of 0.15 in 2020–23 (0.19 alongside the other factors), because daily-return betas understate true betas. Hedged, its Sharpe falls from 0.68 to 0.46.
What's left is earnings news.The remaining premium comes from the industry-level sentiment theme (earnings surprises), not from industry momentum. Industry-level quality was a consistent drag.

Finding · exploratory

The "neutral" book wasn't neutral

Each month the optimiser forced the industry-bet book's estimated beta to zero, and it complied to within 0.000004. Its realised beta was still 0.15 in 2020–23 (0.19, t = 3.9, estimated alongside the other Fama–French factors). Betas estimated from daily returns understate the true beta of less-synchronously traded stocks, and betting on whole industries concentrates the error. The dashed line hedges that exposure using only the book's past 36 months of returns.

Finding · exploratory

Which theme carries the industry premium?

The industry-bet book averages five theme components. Traded one at a time and beta-hedged, the sentiment component (earnings surprises and momentum averaged by industry) is the most persistent contributor. Its correlation with a standard industry-momentum factor is only 0.01–0.24, which points to slowly spreading industry earnings news. Quality at the industry level lost money.

Data honesty

What free data can and can't show

How much of the market the free data sees

Share of large non-financial SEC filers (assets ≥ $1bn) present in the priced universe, relative to 2025. Free prices only cover companies still listed today, so the early years miss many firms that later delisted.

  • Absolute Sharpe ratios are biased upward by survivorship, especially before 2020. The study compares books built on the same universe.
  • Industry codes are current, not historical. Trading costs and borrow fees are modelled, not measured.
  • The five themes are my reconstruction; Vanguard does not disclose its variables. Nothing here describes the fund's own returns.
  • 48 test months cannot detect Sharpe differences much below 0.8. A CRSP replication back to 1990 is the next step.
How it was built
Point-in-time fundamentals2.7M as-first-reported XBRL facts from SEC bulk data. Each number is used only from the day after it was filed.
Peer-relative scores15 characteristics in 5 themes, ranked within FF12, FF49, SIC-3 or return-correlation peer groups.
Beta-neutral optimiserFactor risk model, 200% gross, spread and square-root impact costs, borrow fees, top 1000 US stocks.
Locked test, logged searchPre-registered hypotheses, a test period locked in code, and every experiment and bug fix kept in an append-only log.
Research artifact, not investment advice. Paper-trading results, when added, are simulated. Data: SEC EDGAR (companyfacts, submissions), Yahoo Finance, Kenneth R. French Data Library. Signals are formed at the month-end close and traded at the next day's close.
